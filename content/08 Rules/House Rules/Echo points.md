# Echo Points

> _"Every triumph, every failure, every sacrifice leaves an echo upon the soul. Those who survive long enough learn to draw upon these echoes, recalling their experiences, heritage, and relationships when fate hangs in the balance."_

## Overview

**Echo Points** are a narrative resource that represents a character drawing upon their past experiences, training, heritage, and relationships to overcome challenges.

Unlike Heroic Inspiration, which rewards moments of heroism, Echo Points reward players for embracing their character's identity and the story unfolding around them.

Echo Points may be spent to invoke a character's defining traits or to turn near-failure into success through sacrifice.

---

# Gaining Echo Points

A character gains Echo Points in the following ways:

|Trigger|Echo Gained|
|---|:-:|
|Start of your turn **if you currently have 0 Echo Points**|+1|
|Roll a natural 1 on any d20 Test|+1|
|A significant new enemy or threat enters the encounter|+1|
|You voluntarily choose to fail a Test or Saving Throw|+2|

### Significant Enemy

A significant enemy is one that meaningfully changes the encounter, such as:

- A named villain.

- A new wave of enemies.

- A powerful monster.

- A major escalation determined by the GM.


Minor reinforcements or summoned creatures do not normally grant Echo Points.

---

# Spending Echo Points

A player may spend **1 Echo Point** to invoke one of their character's defining traits.

The player must explain **how** that aspect of their character helps in the current situation.

The GM may reward this by granting:

- Advantage on a roll.

- A bonus to a roll.

- Additional information.

- A narrative benefit.

- Another appropriate effect.


The invoked trait should be relevant to the situation.

---

## Valid Traits

A player may invoke:

- Species

- Background

- Class

- Subclass

- Bond

- Faction Membership

- Profession

- Significant Life Experience

- Other defining character traits approved by the GM


### Examples

**Species**

> "As an elf who has wandered ancient forests for centuries, I recognise these forgotten boundary stones."

**Background**

> "As a former city guardsman, I know where smugglers usually hide contraband."

**Class**

> "Years of studying divine scripture help me identify this forgotten symbol of Thaniel."

**Bond**

> "My Bond III (Loyalty) with Garrick means I know exactly how he fights. I can predict his next move."

---

# Success at a Cost

When a character **fails a d20 Test by 2 or less**, they may spend **1 Echo Point** to propose a meaningful complication that turns the failure into a success.

The player proposes the cost.

The GM may:

- Accept the complication.

- Negotiate a different complication.

- Reject the proposal if it would undermine the story or trivialise the challenge.


The success occurs, but the cost is immediately paid.

---

## Examples of Complications

### Equipment

- Your weapon breaks.

- Your shield splinters.

- Your armour is damaged.

- Your spellcasting focus cracks.

- Your thieves' tools become unusable.


### Physical

- Gain Travel Fatigue.

- Fall prone.

- Lose all remaining movement.

- Take minor damage.

- Become exposed to danger.


### Social

- Owe a favour.

- Reveal sensitive information.

- Damage your reputation.

- Create suspicion.

- Offend an important NPC.


### Narrative

- Reinforcements are alerted.

- Valuable time is lost.

- Supplies are consumed.

- A secondary objective becomes more difficult.

- An enemy escapes while you achieve your goal.


### Magical

- Lose concentration.

- A magic item temporarily ceases functioning.

- Wild magical side effects occur.

- An enchantment is weakened.


---

# Example: Broken Blade

A fighter misses an attack by **1**.

The player says:

> "I'll spend 1 Echo Point. The enemy shatters my sword, but the broken blade continues forward and strikes true."

The GM agrees.

The attack hits.

The enemy falls.

The fighter is left holding only the shattered hilt of their weapon.

---

# Design Philosophy

Echo Points exist to encourage players to think about **who their characters are**, rather than simply what numbers appear on their character sheet.

They reward:

- Embracing character identity.

- Accepting failure as part of the story.

- Making meaningful sacrifices.

- Building relationships.

- Creating memorable moments.


Every Echo Point spent should reinforce the narrative by asking one simple question:

> **"Why does your character succeed here?"**

The answer should always be rooted in the character's history, experiences, or the price they are willing to pay.
