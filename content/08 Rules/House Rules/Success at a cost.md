# Success at a Cost (Complications Rule)

> _"Heroes don't always succeed because they are stronger. Sometimes they succeed because they are willing to pay a price."_

## Overview

Sometimes a character comes agonisingly close to success. Rather than allowing a near miss to become a complete failure, a player may choose to accept a meaningful complication.

When a character **fails a d20 Test by 2 or less**, they may attempt to turn that failure into a success by paying a narrative cost.

The complication should leave a lasting consequence that makes the success memorable.

---

## When It Can Be Used

A player may invoke **Success at a Cost** when:

- They fail an Ability Check, Attack Roll, or Saving Throw by **2 or less**.

- The outcome could reasonably succeed with an appropriate sacrifice.

- The GM agrees that the proposed complication is suitable.


The player proposes the cost.

The GM may:

- Accept the proposal.

- Negotiate a different complication.

- Reject the proposal if it would undermine the story or remove meaningful consequences.


---

## Echo Cost

Invoking **Success at a Cost** requires the expenditure of **1 Echo Point**.

The Echo Point represents the character drawing upon their determination, experience, and resolve to force success despite overwhelming odds.

---

## Types of Complications

A complication should permanently or temporarily change the situation. It should never be a trivial inconvenience.

### Equipment

- A weapon breaks.

- A shield splinters.

- Armour is damaged.

- A spellcasting focus cracks.

- Thieves' tools become unusable.

- Ammunition is exhausted.


### Physical

- Fall prone after succeeding.

- Take minor damage.

- Gain a level of Travel Fatigue.

- Lose all remaining movement.

- Become restrained or exposed.


### Social

- Owe a favour.

- Reveal sensitive information.

- Damage your reputation.

- Create suspicion.

- Offend an important NPC.


### Narrative

- Reinforcements are alerted.

- Valuable time is lost.

- Supplies are consumed.

- Another objective becomes more difficult.

- An enemy escapes while you achieve your goal.


### Magical

- Lose concentration.

- A magical item becomes inert until repaired.

- Magical energy creates an unintended side effect.

- A ward or enchantment is weakened.


---

## Example: Broken Blade

**Situation**

A fighter attacks an enemy commander.

The attack misses by **1**.

The player says:

> "I'll spend 1 Echo Point. My sword breaks against his shield, but the broken blade still drives forward and kills him."

The GM agrees.

**Narration**

The commander catches the blade on his shield and twists violently. Steel snaps with a sharp crack. The broken point, driven by the force of the swing, punches through the gap beneath his arm. He stares in disbelief before collapsing, while the fighter is left holding only the shattered hilt of their weapon.

The attack succeeds.

The weapon is destroyed.

---

## Example: The Impossible Climb

A rogue fails an Athletics check by **2** while climbing a collapsing tower.

The player spends **1 Echo Point**.

> "I make the climb, but I lose my backpack as it falls into the ravine."

The GM accepts.

The rogue reaches the top.

Their equipment is gone.

---

## Example: Winning the Argument

A bard narrowly fails a Persuasion check.

The player spends **1 Echo Point**.

> "The duke agrees to help us—but only if we complete a dangerous task for him first."

The check succeeds.

A new obligation is created.

---

## Design Philosophy

Success at a Cost allows dramatic moments without removing the importance of failure.

The goal is not to negate bad rolls, but to transform them into memorable stories where heroes achieve victory through sacrifice.

Every complication should leave the players thinking:

> _"We succeeded... but was it worth the price?"_
